Networking events vs. referral groups

Both can grow your business. They just do different jobs.

Claim your free seatCall 1-800-481-8638

By Zach Wennstedt, founder · Published November 17, 2026

Short answer: Open networking events are best for meeting many new people fast and testing a market. Referral groups are best for steady, repeat introductions, because the same non-competing members meet on a schedule and learn exactly who to send each other. Most owners do best using events to meet people and a referral group to turn relationships into business.

What is the difference between a networking event and a referral group?

A networking event is open: mixers, chamber after-hours, industry meetups, coworking socials. Anyone can attend, the crowd changes every time, and nobody has agreed to do anything for anyone. A referral group is closed or semi-closed: the same members meet regularly, there is usually one seat per trade, and the stated purpose is to pass each other business.

Neither is better in general. They solve different problems. This post compares the formats, not any particular organization; plenty of good groups and events exist under many names.

When are networking events the better choice?

  • You are brand new. You need to meet a lot of people quickly to learn the local market.
  • You are testing a new niche or city. Events let you sample many industries in one night.
  • You want visibility, partners or hires, not just customers.
  • Your schedule is unpredictable, so a weekly commitment is not realistic yet.

To get value from an event, go with a goal: three real conversations, one follow-up coffee booked. Ask people who their best customers are before you describe yours. And follow up within 48 hours, because the people you met will forget you by the weekend.

When is a referral group the better choice?

  • Your customers find you by word of mouth. Contractors, home services, financial and legal pros, health and wellness, local B2B services.
  • You want repeatable results, not a lucky night.
  • You are willing to give referrals, not just collect them.
  • You can show up consistently. Trust builds with repetition.

The reason referral groups work is simple: people send business to people they know and trust, and trust takes repeated contact. In my years running sales teams for plumbing and remodeling companies, the steady referral sources were always relationships we saw week after week, never someone met once at a mixer.

What does each format really cost in time?

Price is only part of the cost. Time is the bigger one for most owners. A typical evening event can take three hours with travel, and the conversations are mostly first meetings. A referral group asks for a fixed block every week, but each meeting is with people who already know what you do, so less time goes to small talk and more to actual introductions.

Membership costs also vary widely. Some groups charge annual dues and meeting fees; some events are free and others charge admission. Ask any group or event directly what it costs in money and attendance before you commit. For the record, NexGen Lead Groups have no dues and run on donations, and weekly meetings are online, so there is no drive time.

How do you get the most from both?

The strongest approach for many owners is a funnel:

  1. Meet at open events, online communities and industry gatherings.
  2. Qualify the people who serve your customer at a different moment.
  3. Invite the best ones to a 15-minute partner call or to visit your referral group.
  4. Commit to a weekly exchange where introductions flow both ways.

Events fill the top of the funnel. A referral group is where those relationships turn into steady work.

What should you look for in a Denver referral group?

The Denver metro has a wide mix of chambers, industry associations and referral groups, plus a large number of new businesses. Whatever group you visit, use this checklist:

  • Is my trade's seat open, with no direct competitor?
  • Do members serve the same customers I do?
  • Does the meeting time fit my week, every week?
  • Can I visit before committing?
  • Are referrals tracked and thanked, or just talked about?
  • Are the costs, money and attendance, stated up front?
  • Is there an online option for weeks I can't drive across town?

Is online networking as good as in person?

For relationship building, in person has an edge: a handshake and shared coffee build trust quickly. For consistency, online wins, because nobody misses a meeting for traffic or weather. A mix of both covers each weakness. That is why our format pairs a weekly online exchange with a once-a-month in-person option.

How do you choose between them if you only have time for one?

Answer these five questions honestly:

  1. Do most of your customers come from word of mouth? If yes, lean toward a referral group.
  2. Are you new to the area or the industry? If yes, open events help you learn the landscape first.
  3. Can you commit the same hour every week? If no, start with events until your schedule settles.
  4. Are you comfortable giving referrals as well as receiving them? Referral groups require both.
  5. Do you need customers, or partners and hires? Groups are built for customers; events are wider.

If three or more answers point toward a referral group, visit one as a guest before you decide. Watch whether real introductions are passed and whether members follow up, not just whether people are friendly.

What should you bring to your first referral group visit?

  • A one-sentence description of who your best customer is.
  • Three trigger moments your customers say out loud.
  • One specific introduction you want this month.
  • A short list of businesses you could refer to, so you can offer value right away.
  • Your calendar, so you can book a one-to-one with anyone who serves your customer.

Visitors who arrive ready to give are remembered. It's the fastest way to see whether the room is a fit for you and to show the room you'd be a good fit for them.

A note on fit

Some people love the energy of a big room and never want a weekly commitment. Others hate mixers and do their best work in small, steady groups. Both are fine. The best format is the one you'll actually keep doing, because consistency is what turns contacts into referrals.

Can you do both without burning out?

Yes, if you set limits. A sustainable rhythm for many owners is one referral group meeting a week plus one or two open events a month, chosen on purpose rather than out of habit. Before each event, decide who you hope to meet. After each one, move the best contacts into a short partner call. Track where your new customers actually came from for a quarter, then spend more time on whichever source is producing. Let the results, not the calendar invites, decide where your networking hours go.

Questions people ask

Are referral groups better than networking events?

Not always. Networking events are better for meeting many people quickly and testing a market. Referral groups are better for steady, repeat introductions from the same trusted, non-competing members.

How often do referral groups meet?

Many meet weekly, some every other week or monthly. NexGen Lead Groups meet online weekly with an optional in-person meetup once a month.

Do referral groups allow competitors?

Most referral groups hold one seat per trade or specialty so members do not compete for the same referrals. Always ask a group how it handles overlapping trades before you join.

What is the best way to follow up after a networking event?

Send a short personal message within 48 hours that mentions something specific from your conversation, and offer a 15-minute call to learn who their best customers are.
