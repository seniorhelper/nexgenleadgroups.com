How to run a referral group meeting

A tight agenda turns a nice chat into real introductions.

Claim your free seatCall 1-800-481-8638

By Zach Wennstedt, founder · Published November 17, 2026

Short answer: A productive referral group meeting starts on time, follows the same agenda every week, keeps each member’s share to a fixed time, focuses on specific asks and trigger moments, records every referral passed, and ends with clear follow-ups. A strong host keeps it moving; the format does the rest.

Why do most referral group meetings drift?

Meetings drift for predictable reasons: no fixed agenda, open-ended introductions, one member who talks for ten minutes, and nobody writing down who promised what. The fix is structure. When I managed sales teams for plumbing and remodeling companies, the best weekly sales meetings ran on the same skeleton every time, so the energy went into the content instead of figuring out what came next. Referral meetings work the same way.

What is a good referral group meeting agenda?

Here is a 45-minute online format, the length NexGen uses for its weekly exchange. For a smaller group or a quick meeting, shrink each block proportionally; the order matters more than the minutes.

TimeBlockWhat happens
0:00–0:03OpenHost welcomes, states the agenda and the goal for today
0:03–0:08WinsReferrals that closed since last week; thank the giver by name
0:08–0:30Member asksEach member shares one trigger moment and one specific ask, 60–90 seconds each
0:30–0:38SpotlightOne member teaches the group how to spot their customer
0:38–0:43Pass referralsMembers state the introductions they will make this week
0:43–0:45CloseHost reads back commitments, confirms next meeting

What should the host say?

A short script keeps any host, new or experienced, on track. Read it word for word the first few weeks.

Opening (30 seconds): "Welcome. We have 45 minutes and one goal: leave with introductions we can actually make this week. Wins first, then asks, then today's spotlight. Keep asks to 90 seconds; I'll give you a 15-second signal."

Wins prompt: "Who has a referral that turned into business since last week? Tell us who gave it to you."

Ask prompt: "Give us one moment your customer says out loud, and one specific person or business you want to meet."

Close: "Here is what we promised: [read list]. Next meeting same time. Thank you for showing up for each other."

How should members prepare their ask?

Most members default to a sales pitch. A pitch tells people what you sell; an ask tells them what to listen for. Have every member fill this in before the meeting:

My ask this week

Trigger moment: "When you hear someone say ______,"

Who I want to meet: "I'd love an intro to ______ (a name, a company or a specific role)."

Why now: "This matters this week because ______."

At NexGen we call these trigger moments. They are easier to remember than a pitch, and they give other members something to listen for all week.

How do you keep the meeting on time?

  • Start at the posted minute, even if people are missing.
  • Use a visible timer for each ask.
  • Call members in a set order so nobody waits to be picked.
  • Park side conversations: "Great topic, take it to a one-to-one."
  • End on time. People come back to meetings that respect their calendar.

How do you track referrals passed?

If it is not written down, it did not happen. Keep a shared sheet with these columns: date, giver, receiver, customer or contact type, status (introduced, met, booked, closed or not a fit), and a note. Review it in the wins block. Tracking does two things: it rewards givers with public thanks, and it shows which members and pairings are producing. What should happen between meetings?

The meeting is the scoreboard; the game happens between meetings. Encourage three habits:

  1. One-to-ones. Each member meets one other member for 15 to 20 minutes each week to learn their business in depth.
  2. Follow-through within 48 hours on every introduction promised in the meeting.
  3. Report back. A one-line update to the giver when a referral moves forward.

In NexGen groups, the system also suggests a Power Pair for each member, a partner who serves the same customers, so one-to-ones start with the person most likely to trade introductions.

What are common referral meeting mistakes?

  • Pitching instead of asking. The group already knows what you sell. Tell them what to listen for.
  • Asks that are too broad. "Anyone who owns a home" gives people nothing to act on.
  • Skipping the wins block. Public thanks is the fuel that keeps people giving.
  • No written commitments. Promises spoken aloud and never recorded rarely happen.
  • Running long. A meeting that ends late every week loses its best, busiest members first.

What does a strong member spotlight look like?

The spotlight is the block that teaches the group to sell for you. Give one member eight minutes every week, rotating through the roster. The goal is not a sales presentation. It is a short lesson in how to recognize that member's customer. A simple structure:

  1. The customer (1 minute): who they are, in plain words.
  2. The moment (2 minutes): what they say or do right before they need you.
  3. A real story (3 minutes): one job, start to finish, with how the customer found you.
  4. The ask (1 minute): one specific person or business the group can help you reach.
  5. Questions (1 minute).

A good spotlight makes it easy for every other member to recognize that customer for weeks afterward.

How do you handle the tricky moments?

  • A member keeps running long: give the 15-second signal, then thank them by name and move on. Talk privately afterward.
  • Two members overlap: agree on a split before it becomes a conflict, or keep one seat per specialty.
  • Attendance drops: remind members why the group exists, share recent wins, and check whether the meeting time still works.
  • No referrals for weeks: spend a meeting on one-to-ones and sharpen every member's trigger moments. Vague asks are usually the cause.

Host checklist

  • Agenda posted before the meeting.
  • Timer ready, order of members set.
  • Referral log open and shared.
  • Spotlight member confirmed.
  • Commitments read back at close.
  • Recap sent within 24 hours.

What should the weekly recap include?

Send a short recap within a day of every meeting. It keeps promises visible and helps anyone who missed the call. Keep it to five parts: the wins and who gave them, each member's ask in one line, the spotlight member's trigger moments, the introductions promised (giver, receiver, target), and next week's spotlight. A recap that takes the host ten minutes to write can save a dozen introductions that would otherwise be forgotten by Friday.

Questions people ask

How long should a referral group meeting be?

Many groups run 45 to 90 minutes. A weekly online exchange of about 45 minutes works well if it follows a fixed agenda and keeps each member ask to 60 to 90 seconds.

What is a good agenda for a leads group meeting?

Open and state the goal, share wins from last week, take a short ask from each member, give one member a spotlight, state the introductions each member will make, and close by reading back commitments.

What should I say in a referral group introduction?

Share one trigger moment your customer says out loud and one specific person or business you want to meet, rather than a general sales pitch.

How do referral groups track referrals?

Most use a shared log with the giver, receiver, contact type and status. Reviewing it at each meeting rewards givers and shows which pairings produce.

Can a referral group meet online?

Yes. Online meetings make weekly attendance easier and remove drive time. NexGen Lead Groups meet online weekly and offer an optional monthly in-person meetup.
